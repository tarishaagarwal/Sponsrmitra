# SponsrMitra: Feature List

Each feature has a name, a priority tag and a description. Priorities follow the job spec: **Must** means the first release fails without it, **Should** means it follows within the first season, and **Later** means it waits until the pilot proves demand.

## 1. Trust and verification

**F1. Fest profile** (Must)
A page for each fest with its name, college, city, dates, type, expected footfall, audience mix, past editions, past sponsors and social links. It is filled in once and reused in every request, pitch and report, so the fest's credentials never have to be retyped.

**F2. Fest verification** (Must)
A fest is verified through a college email check plus confirmation from a faculty advisor or the student affairs office. Unverified fests can browse but cannot send requests, which keeps fake fests out and gives brands a reason to trust what arrives.

**F3. Brand profile** (Must)
A company page showing category, sponsorship types it supports (cash, in-kind, prizes, stalls, hiring), regions, typical budget band, past college sponsorships, a named contact and a preferred channel. Brands fill it in once and control what students see.

**F4. Contact verification** (Must)
A person checks every contact by test email, call or confirmation from the brand, then stamps it with the check date. Contacts are re-checked every 90 days and removed after a bounce or a wrong-number report.

**F5. Freshness labels** (Must)
Each contact shows how recently it was verified, for example "verified 12 days ago". Contacts older than 90 days turn amber and drop down search results, so students see how much to trust each name.

**F6. Role-based access** (Must)
Each person sees what they need: sponsorship lead, team member, convenor, finance head, faculty viewer, brand manager and alumnus. A convenor can view and approve but not edit outreach; a faculty viewer sees progress only.

**F7. Consent and privacy controls** (Must)
Contact details are stored with consent, brands choose which details are visible, and anyone can ask for their data to be exported or deleted. This protects personal data and is a condition of trust with brands and alumni.

**F8. Report and block** (Should)
Any user can report a fake listing, a wrong contact or abusive behaviour, and a brand can block a fest. Reports go to a review queue with a response target.

## 2. Discovery

**F9. Verified company directory** (Must)
A searchable list of companies that have confirmed they sponsor college fests. Only verified entries appear, so a student never wastes an hour on a dead contact.

**F10. Filters and search** (Must)
Filter by category, region, cash or in-kind, budget band, sponsorship format, fest types previously sponsored, verification freshness and whether an alumnus works there. Filters are saved per fest, so a team returns to the same view.

**F11. Company page** (Must)
One page per company combining its profile, benchmark ranges, alumni connections and the fest's own history with it. It answers "should we approach them, and how" without opening five tabs.

**F12. Shortlists** (Must)
Save companies into named lists such as "Beverages" or "Tech recruiters", assign a list to a team member and share it with the team. A shortlist is the natural starting point of the pipeline.

**F13. Sponsors of similar fests** (Should)
Shows companies that sponsored fests of the same type, size and region, with the deal type but not the private amount. It turns other teams' results into a starting list.

**F14. Recommended sponsors** (Should)
Ranks companies for each fest using simple rules: fest type, size, city, past deals and sponsor category fit. It starts rule-based and improves as more deals are recorded.

**F15. Local and in-kind finder** (Should)
A city-level view of small businesses that give products or services, such as printers, cafes, gyms and coaching centres. This covers the small deals that big platforms ignore.

**F16. Fest directory for brands** (Should)
Brands browse verified fests by city, audience, size, dates and format. They can shortlist fests and invite them to pitch.

## 3. Benchmarks

**F17. Target planner** (Must)
A lead enters fest type, size, city and budget needs and sees the range that comparable fests raised, split into cash and in-kind. It gives a realistic target in minutes instead of a guess.

**F18. Deal benchmark ranges** (Must)
Anonymised ranges for what fests received by size, type and sponsor category. Only ranges are shown, never a named fest's private figure.

**F19. Sample-size guard** (Must)
A range appears only when enough deals sit behind it. Below the threshold the product says "not enough data yet" instead of showing a number that misleads.

**F20. Contribute-your-numbers** (Must)
Fests unlock fuller benchmarks by sharing what they raised and from whom. This gives every team a reason to add data and grows the benchmark set over time.

**F21. Ask calculator** (Should)
Suggests an ask for each sponsor tier, such as title, category and associate sponsors, based on the benchmarks and the fest's own size. The lead can adjust it and see how the total moves against the target.

**F22. In-kind valuation helper** (Later)
Helps a team put a fair rupee value on products and services offered in kind, so a free printing run or a cafe voucher can be compared against a cash cheque.

## 4. Warm introductions

**F23. Alumni network by college** (Must)
Alumni sign up with their college, company and role, and students see alumni at each target company. This is the raw material of every warm introduction.

**F24. Alumni profile** (Must)
Each alumnus states how they can help: introduce, advise, or mentor only. They also choose how many requests they will accept per month.

**F25. Intro request builder** (Must)
A pre-filled message with the fest's name, dates, ask and one line on why this company fits. The lead reviews it and sends it in under two minutes.

**F26. Intro status tracker** (Must)
Shows whether a request was sent, seen, accepted, declined or passed to someone else, and how long it has waited. Silence becomes visible instead of confusing.

**F27. One-tap alumni response** (Must)
An alumnus can accept, decline or suggest someone else from a notification, without logging in or writing a long reply. It keeps the cost of helping under two minutes.

**F28. Intro limits and etiquette** (Should)
Caps how many requests a fest can send to one alumnus and how often, and shows a short etiquette note. This protects alumni goodwill, which the whole feature depends on.

**F29. Alumni thanks and recognition** (Later)
Sends a thank-you and a summary after a deal closes, and shows alumni how many intros they made. Recognition keeps them willing to help next year.

**F30. Peer and senior referral** (Later)
Lets students ask seniors, not only alumni, for an introduction and see which seniors already interned at a company.

## 5. Pitch and outreach

**F31. Sponsorship package builder** (Should)
Lets a team define tiers, benefits, price ranges and a list of visibility assets such as banners, stalls, social mentions and prize naming. The result is a clear menu a sponsor can choose from.

**F32. Pitch deck generator** (Should)
Builds a deck from the fest profile, packages and proof pack, ready to download or share as a link. It removes the blank-page problem for first-time leads.

**F33. Email and message templates** (Should)
Templates for first contact, follow-up, meeting request, thank-you and decline reply. They fill in fest and company details and can be edited before sending.

**F34. Proof pack** (Should)
A place to upload footfall numbers, photos, past sponsor logos, social reach and testimonials, each marked as evidenced or self-reported. Sponsors ask for proof, and this answers them once.

**F35. Pre-send checklist** (Should)
A short check before each request: is the contact fresh, is the ask within the benchmark range, is the proof pack attached, has anyone else approached this company. It prevents avoidable mistakes.

**F36. Meeting scheduler** (Should)
Adds a calendar link to a request so the company can pick a slot, and sends reminders to both sides. A yes turns into a meeting without a long email thread.

**F37. Personalisation prompts** (Later)
Suggests a specific angle for each company, such as a recent campaign or a hiring drive, so the pitch does not read like a template.

## 6. Pipeline and team

**F38. Shared pipeline board** (Must)
A board with stages: shortlisted, intro requested, contacted, replied, meeting, negotiating, committed, paid and declined. Every team member sees the same board.

**F39. Duplicate outreach warning** (Must)
Warns a member before contacting a company that someone else on the team already approached, and shows what was said. It ends the problem of two people sending different asks to one contact.

**F40. Owners, tasks and reminders** (Must)
Each company has an owner, a next step and a due date, with reminders that go to WhatsApp and email. Work stops falling through the gaps during exams.

**F41. Activity log and notes** (Must)
Records every message, meeting, status change and note against the company. A new member can pick up a conversation without asking anyone.

**F42. CSV import and export** (Must)
Teams upload their existing spreadsheet on day one and export everything at any time. The product starts useful instead of empty, and nobody feels locked in.

**F43. Notifications and digests** (Must)
Alerts for replies, intro responses and due tasks, plus a weekly digest for the convenor. They arrive on email and WhatsApp so nobody has to log in daily.

**F44. Team dashboard** (Should)
Shows confirmed, likely and hoped-for money against the target, with counts by stage and by owner. The convenor uses it to decide what to spend.

**F45. Decline reasons** (Should)
When a company says no, the team records why, such as budget, timing or category. Patterns in the reasons show where to change the pitch.

## 7. Closing and delivery

**F46. Commitment record** (Should)
Captures the sponsor, amount or in-kind value, deliverables, dates and contact for each agreed deal. It is visible to the convenor and finance head.

**F47. Agreement templates** (Should)
Plain-language sponsorship agreement templates that the team can fill in. They are a starting point, not legal advice, and the college can add its own terms.

**F48. Deliverables checklist** (Should)
Lists what the fest promised, such as logo placement, stall space, social posts and stage mentions, with an owner and a due date for each. It makes sure promises are kept.

**F49. Payment tracker** (Should)
Tracks amounts, due dates and status for each sponsor, with reminders to the finance head. It records payments but does not move money.

**F50. Sponsor view** (Should)
A shareable link where the sponsor sees the deliverables and their progress. It builds trust and cuts the number of "what is happening" messages.

**F51. Proof of delivery** (Should)
Upload photos, screenshots and numbers against each deliverable. Proof is collected as the fest happens, not scrambled together afterwards.

**F52. Sponsor report generator** (Should)
Assembles a post-fest report from the proof, footfall and social numbers, ready to send. A good report is the strongest case for a renewal.

## 8. Brand side

**F53. Brand dashboard** (Should)
Shows incoming requests, shortlisted fests, decisions made and delivery reports in one place. It gives a busy manager a single view.

**F54. Request inbox** (Should)
Requests arrive in a standard format with fest, ask, audience and proof, so they can be compared. Nothing arrives as a loose PDF.

**F55. One-click response** (Should)
Accept, decline, ask a question or forward to a colleague in one click. Decline reasons flow back to the fest.

**F56. Fest comparison** (Should)
Places two or three fests side by side on audience, size, price and benefits. It helps a manager choose where to place a limited budget.

**F57. Inbound controls** (Should)
Brands set categories, budget bands, regions and a weekly cap, and can pause requests entirely. This keeps the inbox worth reading.

**F58. Campaign tags and budget tracking** (Later)
Lets a brand tag sponsorships to a campaign or hiring drive and track spend against budget.

**F59. Renewal prompts** (Later)
Reminds a brand to review last year's sponsorship before its budget cycle, with the delivery report attached.

## 9. Handover and memory

**F60. Fest archive** (Should)
Stores every company approached, the outcome, the contacts, the terms and the notes for each fest edition. It belongs to the fest, not to one person.

**F61. Sponsor history** (Should)
Shows everything the fest has ever done with a company across editions, including what was promised, delivered and paid. A new lead sees the full relationship at a glance.

**F62. Handover checklist** (Should)
Prompts the outgoing lead to close open threads, update contacts and add the next team as owners. The archive is complete when the team changes.

**F63. One-click renewal** (Later)
Starts next year's request from last year's record, with the delivery report attached and the terms prefilled.

**F64. Template library** (Later)
A shared library of decks, emails and agreements that past teams marked as effective, with the number of replies each earned.

## 10. Admin and operations

**F65. Verification console** (Must)
A queue where the operator verifies fests, brands and contacts, logs the method used and sets the next re-check date. It is the daily tool that keeps the directory trustworthy.

**F66. Data quality checks** (Must)
Flags bounced emails, duplicate companies, stale contacts and missing fields, and routes them to the verification queue. Quality is monitored, not assumed.

**F67. Moderation queue** (Should)
Handles reports, disputes and suspected fraud with a status and an owner for each case.

**F68. Audit log** (Should)
Records who changed which contact, listing or setting, and when. It helps resolve disputes and supports privacy requests.

**F69. Operator analytics** (Should)
Shows sign-ups, requests sent, reply rates, intro acceptance, verification backlog and deals closed. It ties the product to the success measures.

**F70. College partner view** (Later)
Gives a college's student affairs office an overview of its fests' sponsorship activity and outcomes, with approval controls.

## 11. Platform

**F71. Mobile-first web app** (Must)
Every screen works on a phone at 360 px width and on a slow connection, since students work mostly from phones.

**F72. Simple sign-in** (Must)
Sign in with a college email or a phone one-time code, and invite teammates by link. There are no long forms before a student sees value.

**F73. Global search** (Should)
One search box that finds companies, contacts, notes, fests and templates.

**F74. Low-bandwidth and accessibility mode** (Should)
Light pages, readable contrast, keyboard support and screen-reader labels. It keeps the product usable on weak connections and for more people.

**F75. Regional languages** (Later)
Interface and templates in Hindi first, then other regional languages, so teams outside big cities can use the product comfortably.

## 12. Later

**F76. Payment processing** (Later)
Collects and releases sponsorship payments through a payment partner. It is out of scope until trust and volume justify the compliance work.

**F77. AI-assisted matching and drafting** (Later)
Suggests sponsors, drafts personalised pitches and summarises meeting notes, once enough real deals exist to learn from.

**F78. Multi-fest bundles** (Later)
Lets several fests from one college offer a combined package to a brand.

**F79. In-app chat** (Later)
Adds messaging between fests, brands and alumni. It waits because email and WhatsApp already work and chat adds moderation cost.

**F80. Other event types** (Later)
Extends the product to hackathons, conferences and community events after college fests work well.

## Release plan

| Release | What ships | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot (no software) | A group chat, a shared sheet and hand-made shortlists for 3 fests | Test whether brands, alumni and benchmarks hold up |
| Release 1 | All Must features (F1 to F7, F9 to F12, F17 to F20, F23 to F27, F38 to F43, F65, F66, F71, F72) | Discover, get introduced, run a shared pipeline, trust the data |
| Release 2 | Should features across pitch, closing, delivery, brand side and handover | Turn replies into signed, delivered and renewed deals |
| Later | Payments, AI matching, bundles, chat, other event types | Expand only after the core job works |

## Status in the built app

This list is the product plan. The built app covers most of it. Where it differs:

- **Alerts (F40, F43):** reminders and alerts appear inside the app only. Email and WhatsApp delivery is not built.
- **Sign-in and invites (F72):** people sign in with an emailed code (not a phone code), and teammates join with a team code (not an invite link).
- **Partly built:** F8 (reporting works, blocking a fest does not), F21 (a suggested ask per company, not per sponsor tier), F36 (a meeting date with an "add to calendar" link, no company-side booking), F74 (a lite mode and labelled controls, but no full accessibility audit).
- **Not built:** F47 (agreement templates), F73 (one search across everything; only companies are searchable) and every feature tagged Later.

`docs/SETUP.md`, Part 3, lists what each missing piece would take.
